Protection model

Existing immutable Structured SFE architecture records are reused as the approval authority: authorized-refactor-before.json, authorized-refactor-after.json and scoring-parity-proof.json. Their exact bytes remain unchanged.

There was no general reviewed-active-code resolver in the existing protection tests. The unrelated golden-content approved_hashes.yaml mechanism remains untouched. This repair adds a narrow TEST-SIDE resolver, protected_integrity.py, with a SHA-pinned approved_active_integrity_v1.json policy.

The policy has two explicitly separated scopes:
  - Architecture approval: exactly the two active implementation files above.
  - Integrity repair approval: exact before/after hashes for nine test assertion adapters.

The original eight tests now delegate their integrity assertions to this resolver. The newer Structured SFE protection test uses the same boundary, because it also snapshots the active protection-test files. This avoids introducing a fresh recursive hash failure. Their other functional/golden assertions are preserved. The test-source migrations are recorded separately from the two business implementation approvals; they are not unrelated runtime changes.

For an approved active path, the historical manifest must name a reviewed historical/current revision, and the actual file must match the exact approved CURRENT digest. Rollback to the historical bytes fails. Every unlisted path still requires exact historical byte equality. There are no ignored directories, globs, skipped tests, fake hash results or dynamically approved current bytes. The policy itself is SHA-pinned; approval records are hash-verified and their mappings must agree with the policy.

No historical manifest or experiment record is regenerated. Old expected hashes remain intact as provenance. Future reviewed active revisions can use a central versioned approval policy rather than rewriting frozen experiments.
